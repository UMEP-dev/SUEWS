- [change][experimental] Add a minimum velocity scale of 0.5 m s-1 to the aerodynamic resistance on every stability branch (resistance method 2; the legacy methods 1 and 3 are unchanged): r_a uses the effective wind sqrt(U^2 + w_min^2) in place of U, with the larger of w_min and the free-convection velocity w_f of #1956 in place of w_min on unstable steps, so at light wind it no longer grows as 1/U, up to the (z-d)/L clip on stable steps (#1979)
  - The quadrature form follows the generalised velocity scale of Mahrt and Sun (1995, Eqs. 20 and 22), and the value is the globally constant subgrid velocity scale of 0.5 m s-1 they suggest (p. 3040), a spatial scale from 5-30 km aircraft averages used here as a constant for a point model; the friction-velocity calculation is unchanged
  - On unstable steps the larger of w_min and w_f is added rather than both, a departure from Mahrt and Sun: the #1956 resistance is unchanged wherever w_f >= w_min, and a w_f below w_min no longer changes r_a. Adding both cut daytime unstable r_a below 0.5 m s-1 wind by about a quarter across the 21 test sites, cooled the surface by up to 8 K and did not improve the observed sensible or latent heat fluxes on those hours; taking the larger roughly halves the cut and limits the daytime surface cooling there to 3.3 K. r_a has a slope break, not a jump, where w_f crosses w_min
  - The same w_min on both sides of neutral keeps r_a continuous where (z-d)/L changes sign whenever w_f <= w_min (a larger w_f retained by the between-pass relaxation can still make it differ, as before), and at fixed wind and roughness w_min introduces no additional stable-side dip
  - Light-wind outputs and subsequent carried state change in all storage heat methods, by day as well as by night; across the 21 test sites of #1956 the largest stable r_a falls from 11,873 to 612 s m-1, and daytime unstable r_a falls by about 14% below 0.5 m s-1 wind
